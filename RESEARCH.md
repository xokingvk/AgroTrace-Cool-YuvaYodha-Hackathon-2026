# AgroTrace Cool — Research & References

## 1. Research Scope

AgroTrace Cool is a low-cost evaporative pre-cooling prototype with integrated sensing, control, monitoring, and batch-level records for farm-gate / FPO collection-centre use.

Research areas:
- Post-harvest field-heat removal and pre-cooling
- Evaporative cooling principles and environmental limits
- Produce-temperature monitoring
- Temperature and relative-humidity sensing
- Low-cost cooling hardware
- Batch-level monitoring and record keeping
- Farm-gate / FPO cold-chain infrastructure
- Existing Indian and international cooling solutions
- Feasibility of adapting the monitoring approach to larger FPO cooling setups

## 2. Key Research Findings

### 2.1 Pre-cooling is a recognized post-harvest step

Pre-cooling removes field heat from harvested produce before subsequent storage and transport. AgroTrace Cool therefore focuses on **first-mile / farm-gate pre-cooling**, not refrigerated transport or long-term cold storage.

Design basis:

`Harvest → FPO / Collection Centre → Pre-Cooling → Storage / Transport → Market`

### 2.2 Evaporative cooling is condition-dependent

Evaporative cooling depends on the moisture-holding capacity of the surrounding air. Relative humidity is therefore an important operating variable.

AgroTrace Cool measures:
- Ambient temperature
- Relative humidity
- Representative produce temperature

The system should not assume the same cooling performance under every environmental condition.

### 2.3 Measure the produce, not only the chamber

A chamber air-temperature reading does not by itself prove that the produce has cooled to the desired level. AgroTrace Cool uses a **DS18B20 waterproof probe** for a representative produce sample and tracks its temperature during the cooling cycle.

A single probe is treated as a representative measurement, not as the average temperature of the entire batch.

### 2.4 FPO and farm-gate infrastructure is a valid deployment context

Government cold-chain documents recognize farm-level infrastructure and pre-cooling as parts of post-harvest handling. Relevant government guidance also identifies Farmer Producer Organizations among eligible entities for applicable cold-chain projects.

Deployment context:
- FPO collection centres
- Farm-gate handling points
- Shared cooling operations
- Batch-level monitoring during pre-cooling

### 2.5 Existing solutions establish prior art

Existing systems already demonstrate evaporative cooling, IoT-connected cooling, farm-level / FPO-oriented preservation, mobile cooling, and temperature monitoring.

AgroTrace Cool therefore does **not** claim that evaporative cooling or IoT cooling is new.

Intended differentiation:
- Low-cost cooling prototype
- Direct produce-temperature monitoring
- Environmental-condition awareness
- Cooling-cycle decision states
- Water and energy logging
- Batch history in an Android application

## 10. Cooling Model Basis

The Stage-1 simulation should use established evaporative-cooling principles.

Core concept:

`Wet-bulb depression = Ambient dry-bulb temperature − Wet-bulb temperature`

Higher available wet-bulb depression generally indicates greater evaporative-cooling potential, while high relative humidity reduces evaporation potential.

The simulation should remain physically constrained and should not assume an arbitrary fixed temperature drop.

## 11. What the Research Supports

The researched sources support these project positions:

- Pre-cooling is a recognized part of post-harvest temperature management.
- Evaporative cooling is an established low-cost cooling approach.
- Environmental conditions affect evaporative-cooling performance.
- IoT-based agricultural temperature monitoring is an established research area.
- Indian government programmes recognize farm-level and FPO-linked cold-chain infrastructure.
- Existing Indian and international projects demonstrate evaporative cooling and IoT monitoring.

Therefore, AgroTrace Cool should be presented as an **engineering integration and validation project**, not as an invention of evaporative cooling.

## 12. What Still Requires Validation

Literature does not automatically prove the performance of our specific prototype.

We still need to establish experimentally:
- Actual cooling rate
- Actual temperature reduction
- Actual water consumption
- Actual energy consumption
- Sensor placement
- Suitable target range for the selected produce
- Repeatability
- Performance under changing humidity
- Suitability for FPO operating conditions

These are prototype-validation questions.

## 13. Intended Research Gap

Existing work demonstrates:
- Evaporative cooling
- Low-cost cooling chambers
- IoT cooling systems
- Decentralized farm-level preservation
- Mobile cooling approaches

AgroTrace Cool focuses on the narrower operational layer:

> **Making a low-cost farm-gate cooling process measurable at batch level through direct produce-temperature monitoring, environmental sensing, operating decisions, resource logging, and a simple Android record system.**

## 14. Limitations and Honest Positioning

### Current stage

**Prototype**

Not yet:
- Full commercial deployment
- Large-scale FPO installation
- Industrial cold room
- Refrigerated transport solution
- Complete cold-chain replacement

### Future path

`Prototype → Field Test → FPO Pilot → Scale-Up`

Scale-up requires validation of:
- Chamber size
- Airflow
- Wet-pad area
- Fan capacity
- Pump capacity
- Water distribution
- Sensor count and placement
- Power demand
- Operating cost
- Hygiene and maintenance
